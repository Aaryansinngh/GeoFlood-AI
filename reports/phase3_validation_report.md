## 1. Executive Summary

GeoFlood AI is an artificial-intelligence geospatial system designed for flood susceptibility mapping, coordinate-level risk interrogation, and scenario simulation across the Brahmaputra floodplain in Assam, India. 

Phase 3 upgrades GeoFlood AI from an interactive geospatial demonstration into a **scientifically defensible, research-grade analytical platform**. To satisfy rigorous peer-review and Earth Observation standards, this evaluation:
1. **Audits Data Leakage:** Benchmarks the spatial autocorrelation gap between naive random splitting and spatially disjoint holdout blocks.
2. **Implements Four Formal Evaluation Protocols:** Naive Random Stratified Holdout ($N=25,830$), Disjoint Spatial Block Holdout ($N=39,749$), Spatially Buffered Holdout ($1.0\text{ km}$ buffer, $N=35,815$), and Cross-Event Temporal Holdout ($N=172,199$).
3. **Calibrates Probabilistic Uncertainty:** Employs Platt Scaling and Isotonic Regression (fitted strictly on validation data) to reduce the Neural Network's Expected Calibration Error (ECE) from **0.310 to 0.055**, achieving a positive Brier Skill Score ($\text{BSS} = \mathbf{+0.180}$).
4. **Discloses Generalisation Boundaries:** Discovers and documents a catastrophic temporal generalisation collapse in Random Forest models ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.901 \rightarrow 0.485$), while demonstrating that the PyTorch Neural Network retains generalisation ($\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.672$).
5. **Establishes Satellite Provenance:** Clarifies that the Sentinel-1 SAR module's $0.991$ IoU metric is an algorithmic proof-of-concept benchmark on a calibrated bimodal Gaussian distribution, not raw satellite ground truth.

All models, data splits, software environments, and evaluation metrics are fully reproducible via `reports/phase3_reproducibility.json`.

## 2. Evaluation Protocols & Baselines

### Evaluation Protocols
* **Protocol 0 — Naive Random Stratified Holdout (Leaked Baseline):** A standard 15% random sample across the full spatial domain. Benchmarks the degree of metric inflation caused by Tobler's First Law of Geography (neighboring $250\text{ m}$ cells sharing identical attributes).
* **Protocol 1 — Spatial Block Holdout (Primary Benchmark):** The geographic region is divided into a $3 \times 4$ grid of 12 contiguous rectangular blocks. Blocks $0, 1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9$ form the training set ($N=92,471$), blocks $5, 11$ form the validation set ($N=39,979$), and blocks $2, 7, 10$ form the held-out test set ($N=39,749$). No test block pixels are ever seen during feature scaling, model fitting, threshold tuning, or probability calibration.
* **Protocol 2 — Spatially Buffered Holdout ($1.0\text{ km}$ Boundary Exclusion):** Test blocks are eroded by a $1.0\text{ km}$ buffer around training boundaries, removing $3,934$ boundary cells to eliminate spatial edge leakage ($N=35,815$).
* **Protocol 3 — Cross-Event Temporal Holdout (Event 2 — DFO_4288):** Models trained on the June–July 2015 flood are evaluated on the subsequent August–September 2015 flood ($N=172,199$ valid cells) with completely independent meteorological and hydrological forcing.

### Reference Baselines
* **Majority Class Baseline:** Always predicts class 0 (dry land). By definition achieves $\text{Recall} = 0.0$, $\text{Precision} = 0.0$, $\text{ROC-AUC} = 0.50$, and $\text{Specificity} = 1.0$.
* **Climatology / Prevalence Baseline:** Always predicts the historical base-rate prevalence $P(Y=1) = \bar{y}$. Achieves $\text{Brier Skill Score} = 0.0$, establishing the minimum threshold for predictive value.

## 4. In-Depth Empirical Findings

### 1. The Spatial Autocorrelation Leak Gap
When evaluating models using naive random splitting, performance appears solid across all models. However, when tested on disjoint spatial blocks, the PR-AUC for Random Forest drops from $0.592$ to $0.507$, and uncalibrated Neural Network Brier Score suffers. Spatial block holdout provides the only true test of whether the model has learned geomorphic hydrology rather than memorizing spatial coordinates.

### 2. Probability Calibration & Reliability
The uncalibrated Neural Network suffers from severe over-confidence: its raw output probabilities yield an Expected Calibration Error (ECE) of **0.310** and a negative Brier Skill Score ($\text{BSS} = -0.947$), meaning its raw probabilities are less accurate than simply predicting the historical climatological mean.

By applying **Platt Scaling** (fitted strictly on the validation block partition):
* **ECE drops from 0.310 to 0.055** (an $82.3\%$ reduction in calibration error).
* **Brier Score drops from 0.165 to 0.0695**, surpassing the Climatology baseline ($0.0848$).
* **Brier Skill Score increases to +0.180**, confirming substantial probabilistic predictive skill.
* Precision at the operating threshold increases from $22.0\%$ to **$57.5\%$**.

### 3. Tree Ensemble Temporal Collapse vs Neural Generalisation
In Protocol 3 (evaluating models on the independent August 2015 flood event, DFO_4288):
* **Random Forest completely collapses** to an ROC-AUC of **0.485** (worse than a coin flip) and Recall of $26.0\%$. This failure occurs because orthogonal decision tree partitions split on localized event-specific rainfall values ($> 350\text{ mm}$), creating brittle spatial lookup heuristics that fail when storm centers shift.
* **PyTorch Neural Network preserves generalisation**, achieving an ROC-AUC of **0.672** and Recall of **80.4%**. The continuous multi-layer perceptron architecture learns smooth, monotonic relationships (higher rainfall + lower relative elevation $\rightarrow$ increased flood risk), demonstrating superior scientific transferability across distinct monsoonal pulses.

## 6. Sentinel-1 SAR Remote Sensing Audit

* **Module Status:** Verified algorithmic demonstration of Otsu thresholding on microwave backscatter.
* **Optimal Threshold:** $t^* = -4.78\text{ dB}$ on $\Delta \sigma^0$.
* **Audit of 0.991 IoU:** The reported IoU of $0.9910$ was generated on a calibrated bimodal Gaussian distribution with low variance ($\sigma \approx 1.5\text{ dB}$). It reflects mathematical convergence of Otsu variance maximization, not empirical satellite sensor accuracy.
* **Real-World Factors:** Real C-band SAR achieves $0.70–0.85$ IoU due to Bragg surface wind scattering, double-bounce in riparian vegetation, and radar shadowing in mountainous margins.

## 7. Scenario Simulation Monotonicity & Extrapolation Safety

* **Monotonicity:** In $99.8\%$ of evaluated floodplain cells, increasing rainfall scenarios produce monotonically increasing flood susceptibility probabilities.
* **Extrapolation Guards:** The simulator enforces input bounds ($\le 800\text{ mm}$ for `rain_event_sum`) to prevent non-physical step responses in Random Forests and uncontrolled saturation in neural networks.

## 9. Viva / Defense Q&A Quick Reference

**Q1: Why did your Random Forest achieve 0.901 ROC-AUC on spatial holdout but only 0.485 on the cross-event test?**  
*Answer:* Decision trees partition feature space with orthogonal axis-aligned splits on localized rainfall thresholds. When storm centers shifted in the subsequent event, those thresholds acted as incorrect spatial filters. In contrast, our PyTorch Neural Network learned smooth, continuous geomorphic representations, maintaining an ROC-AUC of 0.672 and Recall of 80.4%.

**Q2: Is GeoFlood AI an early-warning flood forecasting tool?**  
*Answer:* No. GeoFlood AI is an event-based flood susceptibility and retrospective inundation mapping system. It utilizes `rain_event_sum` (total storm precipitation), which is only fully observed post-event. For early warning, the architecture can ingest numerical weather prediction forecasts, but this requires separate calibration.

**Q3: Is the 0.991 SAR IoU an empirical satellite accuracy measurement?**  
*Answer:* No. The 0.991 IoU is an internal algorithmic benchmark verifying Otsu thresholding on an ideal bimodal C-band backscatter simulation. In real-world satellite deployments, wind-induced Bragg scattering and vegetation double-bounce constrain empirical SAR IoU to 0.70–0.85.

**Q4: How did you eliminate data leakage during model training and evaluation?**  
*Answer:* We enforced four strict boundaries: (1) Scalers, threshold tuners, and probability calibrators were fitted exclusively on training or validation blocks; (2) Spatial blocks and 1km buffer zones separated train from test cells; (3) Permanent water was excluded from feature inputs; and (4) The cross-event evaluation used an independent future flood event.
